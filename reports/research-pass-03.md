# Research pass 03 — 2026-08-22

This pass adds 30 deliberately mixed candidates: 15 Collletttivo families, five OSP families with verified local packages, six additional OSP references, one open Insular text face, and three Gaelic references. Twenty-one new families render from local files, bringing the lab to 119 candidates and 45 local specimens.

## Strongest candidates

- **Editorial / literary:** Sprat is the broadest new working system because its weight and width range can move from narrow text hierarchy to extended display. Ortica is the sharper literary voice. Libertinage is the most conceptually useful editorial experiment: its A–Z variants turn family selection into an indexing system.
- **UI / body:** Absans is the best quiet utility face in this pass because it remains visibly constructed at small sizes. Ronzino is useful when office-software familiarity is part of the material. Apfel Grotezk is the more vernacular interface option.
- **Technical / system:** Necto Mono is the cleanest practical addition. NotCourierSans has the strongest open-computing provenance. OSP-DIN is the best signage and engineering reference.
- **Experimental:** Aujournuit's width axis changes the density of its calligraphic gesture. Belgica Belgika makes collaborative iteration part of the family. Sans Guilt is valuable as a record of workshop tools and translation, even while it remains reference-only.
- **Archival / institutional:** Cianán Ùr is the strongest new documentary face. Fluxisch Else keeps the evidence of scanning and reconstruction visible. Glanchló GC is the deepest Gaelic book reference but cannot be bundled under its current terms.
- **Early digital / internet:** NotCourierSans and Necto Mono supply two distinct computer voices: collaborative FLOSS mono and sparse early-screen plainness. Reglo is worth revisiting if its package gains a complete license file.
- **Display / atmospheric:** Sinistre is the standout. Mazius Display's extra italic gives a different, unstable formal register. Coconat and Ribes add soft and organic mass without relying on effects.
- **Vernacular:** Messapia has a specific southern Italian inscriptional source. Cianán Ùr comes from older Irish print rather than generalized “Celtic” decoration. Fluxisch Else makes production history part of its surface.
- **Wildcards:** Sneaky Times hides structural interference inside a familiar reading texture. Libertinage offers 27 editorial mutations. Cowboy Henk is a promising Belgian comics reference pending a clean package inspection.

## Celtic / Insular shortlist

- **Sinistre:** the strongest open display choice. Its `wght` axis moves between regular, bold, and dark states, and its Book of Kells, stone-carving, uncial, Roman, and Fraktur sources are integrated rather than pasted on.
- **Cianán Ùr:** the strongest open text choice. It is calmer, older-print-oriented, and much more plausible for captions, short passages, records, or literary material.
- **Glanchló GC:** the strongest restricted reference for serious Gaelic text. Its Petrie and Colum Cille lineage, small caps, italics, and Insular alternates make it worth contacting the designer for an appropriate license.
- **Bunchló Nua GC / Saorchló GC:** useful secondary references for Gaelic book and display voices. They remain unbundled until their individual package terms are confirmed.

## Variable and OpenType behavior

- Sinistre's source defines a `wght` axis; the local WOFF2 is exposed from 400–900.
- Aujournuit's source defines a `wdth` axis; its airy, condensed, dense, regular, and wide static states remain beside the local variable file.
- Sprat supplies weight and width interpolation plus 18 static width/weight combinations.
- Glanchló GC documents small caps and alternate Insular `r` and `s`, but it remains reference-only.
- NotCourierSans includes two private-use ornaments reachable through its ornament feature.

## Licensing problems

- Gaelchló's Glanchló package explicitly prohibits redistribution without permission. It permits free use for Celtic-language material and non-commercial material in other languages, while commercial non-Celtic publication requires a license. The inspected files were not retained.
- Reglo states OFL in its FONTLOG but does not include the full license text. Erbarre asserts OFL in source metadata but likewise lacks a complete bundled license. Both remain references.
- Sans Guilt's current archive endpoint did not produce a verifiable package. Le Patin Helvète and Cowboy Henk still need package-level binary and license inspection.
- Collletttivo's downloaded repositories include `LICENSE.txt`; Cianán Ùr includes `OFL.txt`; the five retained OSP packages include OFL or GPL-with-font-exception terms.

## Purchase, discard, and systems

No purchase is required for the strongest additions. The one licensing conversation worth having is **Glanchló GC** if a commercial non-Celtic project needs its exact Gaelic authority.

Deprioritize without deleting: **Ronzino** when Arial-like familiarity is irrelevant; **Halibut** when Sprat already covers the required width experiment; **Reglo** until its package is licensing-complete; **Logisoso** until a usable compiled font returns to the repository.

Systems worth testing:

- **Sinistre + Absans + Necto Mono:** Insular display, quiet interface text, dry technical data.
- **Cianán Ùr + Apfel Grotezk:** documentary Irish serif with rough commercial navigation.
- **Sprat + NotCourierSans:** variable editorial structure with open-computing metadata.
- **Aujournuit + Ronzino + OSP-DIN:** gestural display, familiar body/UI, engineered labels.
- **Ortica + Fluxisch Else:** sharp literary serif against visibly reconstructed institutional sans.

No final family is selected.
